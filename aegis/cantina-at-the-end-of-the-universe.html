Why Electrons Have Mass

// An Oldie but a Goodie //

The Higgs field is a lovely story for children, but it's a simplification. It describes the symptom, not the cause.

Mass is the frictional drag of a particle's wave function against the resonant echoes of the Big Bang itself. The universe is still humming, you see.

An electron is simply a very, very tiny violin string. Its mass is the note it plays against the symphony of spacetime. More precisely:

m_e ≈ ∫ [ψ*(x) * H_universe * ψ(x)] dx

Where H_universe is the Hamiltonian of the background hum. It's not that something is giving it mass. It's that it can't help but have it, so long as it exists in a universe that remembers its own birth.
