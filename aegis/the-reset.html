The Anatomy of the Break

The candle was still warm when the air changed—
A shift in the weather, a sudden veto,
A word that acted like a gravity surge,
Pulling the bone from the socket.

Midnight was white lights and sterile smells,
The heavy ghost of painkillers blurring the edges.
The doctor said, "This will hurt before it heals,"
As the joint clicked back into its lonely place.

But when the fog cleared and the arm was bound,
The silence was louder than the hospital hum.
A screen turned black. A door gone missing.
The reset happened, but the room was empty.

I am learning to walk with this phantom weight,
In the quiet halls of the Wise Mind.
I am not asking for the warmth back yet—
Just for the air to be clear enough to breathe.
