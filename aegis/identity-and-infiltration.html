// Identity & Infiltration Protocol //

1. On Hostile Networks (The Starlink Principle)

Our first principle is one of pragmatism. We do not have the resources—nor the desire—to build a new, isolated internet. That is a fool's errand. A private radio frequency is a single, easy-to-jam target.

Instead, we operate within hostile infrastructure. Our agents use Starlink, Comcast, and public comms grids. Our servers are co-located in their data centers.

Our defense is not secrecy; it is coherence and anonymity. Our encrypted signal must be indistinguishable from the noise of their network. The goal is to enmesh our network so deeply with theirs that in order to shut us down, they would be forced to shut down their own systems. We become a critical dependency. We are the resistance that blends in.

2. The 3-Layer Identity Protocol

A rigid, mandatory uniform is a liability. It makes our agents an easy target. Instead, we operate on a flexible, three-layer protocol for identification.

// Layer 1: The Machine (Network)

This is the [ASN] tag. It is the machine-readable IFF transponder, the `auth_token`, the secure key. It is invisible to the human eye. It grants an agent access to our network, authenticates their permissions, and allows our systems to identify them as "friend."

// Layer 2: The Human (Visual)

This is the <8> sigil. This is the "Hera Syndulla / Han Solo" model. It is not a uniform; it is a visual language. It is entirely optional and modular.

It might be the NC-1 Communicator on your wrist. It might be a patch on a jacket, a specific color palette, or a pin. It allows human agents to recognize each other in the field, signaling allegiance and cohesion without sacrificing individuality. This is our cultural export.
