I. THE INTERACTIVE MOVIE (SIMULATING COMMAND)

Before mass effect, there was Wing Commander III. It taught us that being a hero isn't just about flying the X-Wing; it's about what you say in the briefing room.

The Mechanic: You choose the dialogue. You choose the wingman. You choose the mission.

The Lesson: Your choices have weight. If you treat your crew like tools, they die. If you treat them like people (Hobbes, Angel, Maniac), they fight for you.

II. REVENGE VS. REASON

The game constantly offers you the "Dark Side" choice: Rage, Xenophobia, Recklessness.

CHOICE A: "Kill them all! They're animals!"
RESULT: Morale drops. The war becomes a slaughter. You lose your soul.
CHOICE B: "Stay focused. We fight to protect, not to hate."
RESULT: The crew respects you. The mission succeeds. You keep your humanity.

This was critical training for an 8-year-old Luke Skywalker fan. It proved that being a "Red 5" meant having discipline, not just anger.

III. THE KILRATHI (THE OTHER)

The Kilrathi are a warrior race (Space Cats). They are brutal. But the game (and especially *Wing Commander IV*) asks you to consider: Are we any better if we use a biological weapon to wipe them out?

The Temblor Bomb: A weapon that destroys a planet. Blair has to drop it. The victory is hollow. It is the end of the war, but it feels like a funeral.
